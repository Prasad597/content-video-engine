[HOOK]
Two threads are updating the same Java Map at the same time.

Which one would you trust?

HashMap?

Or ConcurrentHashMap?

[REVEAL]
For concurrent access, ConcurrentHashMap is the safer choice.

But why?

[HASHMAP_PROBLEM]
A regular HashMap is not thread-safe.

If multiple threads modify it without proper synchronization, you can get race conditions and inconsistent results.

[OBVIOUS_FIX]
So why not just put one big synchronized lock around the HashMap?

That can make access safe, but now threads may have to wait for the same lock.

ConcurrentHashMap is designed specifically for concurrent access.

[CONCURRENT_MAP]
It allows multiple threads to perform many operations concurrently instead of forcing every operation through one single map-wide lock.

[IMPORTANT_DETAIL]
But don't misunderstand this.

ConcurrentHashMap does not magically make a sequence of multiple operations atomic.

For compound actions, use its atomic methods such as putIfAbsent, compute, or merge when appropriate.

[INTERVIEW_RULE]
Interview rule:

Single-threaded use? HashMap is usually enough.

Shared concurrent access?

Use ConcurrentHashMap when you need its concurrency guarantees.
